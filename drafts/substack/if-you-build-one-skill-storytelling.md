# If you only build one skill, build this one

### Why storytelling is the foundation, and a simple way to start

---

Last time, I made the case that the skills worth developing now are the ones AI cannot fake, and I asked you to pick just one to start building. If you wrote to me and asked which one I would choose, here is my honest answer.

Start with storytelling.

Not because it is the flashiest, but because it is the foundation the other skills stand on. Leadership is mostly storytelling: helping a group see where you are going and why it matters. Presence is largely storytelling: knowing what you are actually there to say. Even critical thinking becomes useful to other people only when you can tell the story of how you got to your conclusion. Build storytelling, and everything else you build has a way to reach people.

There is also a simpler reason. A machine can generate a thousand competent stories in a second. What it cannot do is tell yours. It has never lived a moment of your life. The one story no AI can produce is the true one only you were there for, and that is exactly the kind that moves people.

## First, kill the myth

The biggest thing standing between most professionals and storytelling is a wrong definition of it.

People hear "tell a story" and picture a natural performer holding a room spellbound with some dramatic, larger-than-life tale. So they conclude they are not that, and they retreat to bullet points.

But storytelling is not charisma, and it is not drama. It is structure plus a true moment. Those are both things you can learn. The most powerful stories in professional life are usually small, quiet, and completely ordinary on the surface. What makes them land is not size. It is that they are real, and that they are shaped.

## A simple way to shape one

Here is the method I come back to again and again. Three moves, and you can build a story with them in about a minute.

**1. Find the real moment.** Not your most impressive achievement, your most honest one. A specific instant where something shifted: a decision you were unsure about, a moment you changed your mind, a small thing that turned out to matter. Honest beats impressive every time, because people believe honest.

**2. Show the gap.** A story is not a flat summary. It moves between what was and what could be. Here is where things stood. Here is what became possible. That contrast is the entire engine of a story. Without it you have a status update. With it you have tension, and tension is what keeps people listening.

**3. Make them the hero.** The point of your story is almost never to prove how good you are. It is to help the other person see something they can use. Tell it so that they, not you, walk away with something. Before anyone follows your idea, they quietly decide whether they trust you, and generosity is how trust gets built.

That is it. A real moment, a gap, and a reason for the listener to care.

## One rep for this week

Take something you did in the last month, anything, and tell it in exactly three sentences:

1. **Here is how things were.** (the situation, honestly)
2. **Here is what changed, and the part I played.** (the moment, the gap)
3. **Here is why it mattered.** (what the listener can take from it)

Say it out loud. Notice how different it feels from listing what you did. That difference, the shift from reporting to storytelling, is the whole skill in miniature. Do it once and you are already ahead of nearly everyone who still believes they are "just not a storyteller."

You are not a natural at this yet. Neither was anyone you admire. It is a skill, which means it is yours to build, one small true story at a time.
