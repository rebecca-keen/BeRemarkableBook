# The skills AI can't fake

### The future belongs to the people who keep developing the human ones

---

A while ago I worked with someone who was, by every technical measure, excellent at their job. They knew their work cold. And yet in the moments that mattered most, explaining their thinking to a room, holding their ground in a hard conversation, making people care about what they had built, something fell flat.

It was not a confidence problem, and it was not a polish problem. It was a skill they had simply never developed. No one had taught them how, and they had never set aside the time to build it. Once they did, and it took real practice, not a pep talk, the difference was obvious to everyone around them.

That is the work I care about: helping people actually develop the human skills, not just perform them.

## AI raised the bar on what "skilled" means

For years, a lot of what we called skill was really a proxy. Producing a clean document, a tidy summary, a competent first draft took time and effort, so we treated the output as proof of ability.

AI just made all of that instant and free. A machine can now generate the competent-looking version of almost anything in seconds. Which means the bar for what counts as a real, valuable skill has moved. The skills that still matter are the ones a model cannot do for you:

- **Storytelling.** Making people understand and care about what you know.
- **Leadership.** Moving a group through uncertainty, not just managing tasks.
- **Presence.** How you show up in a room, especially under pressure.
- **Critical thinking.** Asking the right question, not just producing an answer.

Here is the part people miss: these are not personality traits you either have or you do not. They are skills. Which means they can be developed, deliberately, by anyone willing to do the work.

## Skills are built, not born

The most limiting belief I run into is "I am just not a natural at this," as if storytelling or presence were gifts handed out at birth.

They are not. They are trainable. Every skill on that list has mechanics you can learn and reps you can put in. The people who seem effortlessly compelling almost always built that ability, whether they realized it or not. And that is genuinely good news: if it is a skill, it is developable, and the distance between where you are and where you want to be is just practice you have not done yet.

That is the whole premise of my work. I do not give career advice, and I do not fix resumes. I help people develop the specific human skills that make them effective and hard to replace, and I help them find the gaps worth closing first.

## One skill to start developing this week

Pick one skill from that list, the one you most wish you were better at. Then make it small and concrete:

1. **Name the specific version of it you need.** Not "be a better communicator," but "explain my work so a non-expert cares within two minutes."
2. **Find one real rep this week.** A meeting, a message, a conversation where you deliberately practice that exact thing.
3. **Notice what happened.** What worked, what felt hard. That reflection is where the skill actually starts to form.

One rep will not transform you. But skills are built one deliberate rep at a time, and most people never take the first one. Taking it puts you ahead of nearly everyone still waiting to feel ready.

That is what Be Remarkable is about: developing the substance that makes you stand out when AI is everywhere. If that is the kind of growth you are after, you are in the right place.
