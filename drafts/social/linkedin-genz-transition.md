# LinkedIn post — Gen Z workplace-readiness angle

Business Insider ran a piece this week on something employers keep telling me privately: Gen Z is having a harder time with the jump from college to work.

Not because they aren't smart. They are.

The reporting points to communication and professionalism as the sticking points, after high school years upended by the pandemic and relationships increasingly mediated by screens. And here is the part that makes it worse: companies are pulling back on training and mentorship at the exact moment new professionals need it most.

So the message to a 22-year-old is basically: figure out the human skills on your own, and do it fast, while AI raises the bar on everything else.

I have spent fifteen years working on precisely these skills, and I will say the thing no one seems to say out loud:

Communication, presence, and professionalism are not personality traits. They are skills. Which means they can be taught, practiced, and built, by anyone willing to do the work.

That is the whole reason I built Be Remarkable. Not to teach polish. To help people actually develop the human skills that make them effective, especially the ones AI cannot replace, and especially for people stepping into their first real roles without a mentor in their corner.
